National AI Badges for Individuals

A national system to discover, assess and formally recognise AI researchers, AI engineers and data scientists — taken from concept to first exam in under a year.

The problem

Talent was there — but invisible, unverified and unrecognised.

Discovery gap

No unified platform or measurable standard made national talent visible to the market.

Market gap

Hiring decisions rested on CVs with no scientific verification of skill.

Motivation gap

No formal recognition tied career progress to skill — pushing talent abroad.

How it was built

Eight months, five phases.

The system

Three tracks, three levels, one standard.

AI Researcher

Weighted rubric: research 55%, recognition 15%, innovation & impact 20%, community 10%. Reviewed by an international committee.

Assistant · Associate · Expert

AI Engineer

Professional exam plus qualifications, experience and a certificate matrix mapped to the 14 competencies.

Assistant · Associate · Expert

Data Scientist

Same methodology: exam, weighted evaluation, three-year validity, two attempts per year, open to all nationalities.

Assistant · Associate · Expert
Results

What happened.

  • First computer-based exam held 25 July 2026 at King Saud University; 75 candidates sat, mean 92.3, 42.7% scored 100+.
  • Batch two closed with 5,319 unique applicants — 3,554 AI engineers and 1,876 data scientists; 877 hold a master's or PhD.
  • Governance adopted: 3-year validity, appeals, ethics charter, annual review; secretariat and minutes in place.
  • Researcher track: ~18 complete dossiers under review by the international committee.
What I took from it

Three lessons.

  • Credibility is built before launch: committees of recognised experts and an independently reviewed item bank did more for trust than any campaign.
  • A 42-day plan with six parallel workstreams held only because governance decisions were made first.
  • Demand was underestimated: batch two was 50× batch one. Capacity and pricing now drive the roadmap.
